# Personal synthetic voice for people losing speech: evidence notes (as of 17 Nov 2026)

**Method and caveats.** I searched 30+ queries and opened about 50 URLs, mostly official product, company, clinical and paper pages. Quoted passages are as returned by the WebFetch extraction step, so check exact wording against the live page before relying on it. The rest of the search-result URLs were not opened and are listed as snippet-only in section 9. No browser tool was available (see section 8). Nothing here is a recommendation.

## 1. Products and services

**Apple Personal Voice (iOS/iPadOS/macOS, on device)**
- Apple Support, "Create a Personal Voice" (https://support.apple.com/HT213878, "Published Date: October 13, 2025"): steps are Settings > Accessibility > Personal Voice. The user "read[s] a series of sentences aloud". No count or duration is stated. Recording can be paused and resumed. Requirements are iOS 17 or later, iPadOS 17 or later, or macOS Sonoma 15 or later, and iPhone 12 or later, iPad Air (5th gen) or later, or an Apple-silicon Mac. A device passcode, Face ID or Touch ID must be set up. The page says "Your recorded speech is then processed securely on device." Languages: "works only in a supported language", linking to a feature-availability list that was not opened.
- Apple Newsroom, 30 Nov 2023 (https://www.apple.com/ca/newsroom/2023/11/a-disability-advocate-preserves-his-voice-with-iphone): "by following a series of text prompts to capture 15 minutes of audio." Also: "train neural networks entirely on-device to advance speech accessibility while protecting users' privacy." Languages are not listed.
- Apple's 2023 phrase count: TechRadar's iOS 17 beta hands-on (search summary only, not opened) reported 150 phrases. Apple's own pages do not state a count.
- Language: a TechCrunch snippet (search result, not opened) says English only at launch. A March 2026 Apple Discussions user report (https://discussions.apple.com/thread/256263339) describes a language picker with only Chinese, Spanish and US English. This is one user's report, not Apple documentation.
- Italian: not verified.

**Microsoft (Azure AI Speech, "personal voice")**
- Microsoft Learn overview (https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/personal-voice-overview; ms.date 2026-09-09, updated 2026-09-16): "a 5–90-second human speech sample", training "Less than 5 seconds", "about 100 languages". "Access to the API is restricted to eligible customers and approved use cases." The product is for businesses building apps: "Business customers to build an app to allow their users to create and use their own personal voice."
- Transparency note (https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/responsible-ai/speech-service/text-to-speech/transparency-note; ms.date 2026-03-31, updated 2026-06-20): "Personal voice requires at least 10 seconds of training data as minimum to create a voice." This conflicts with the 5-second figure above (see section 6). Accessibility is among the approved use cases ("to facilitate communication by people with speech impairments"). Watermarks are added to personal-voice output.
- Language support (https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/language-support?tabs=custom-tts; ms.date 2026-10-28, updated 2026-11-12): the "Personal voice" table includes `it-IT` Italian (Italy). This is the only Italian personal-voice support confirmed from an official page in this research.
- Speak For Me with Team Gleason: a 2024 preview (search summaries of Engadget and similar coverage, not opened). No 2026 status found. Not verified.

**ModelTalker (University of Delaware, free for ALS/MND on request)**
- Home and register pages (https://www.modeltalker.org/, https://www.modeltalker.org/register-with-modeltalker/; © 2026 footer, no update date). Registration categories include "Voice Banker or Recipient", "Proxy Voice Banker" ("Someone who is recording to provide a voice for another specific individual who is unable to bank his or her own voice"), "Voice Donor", and "Clinician". Donors and clinicians are "no charge".
- Sentence counts (news items on the same site): "a full 1600- or 3200-sentence inventory" (2017–2019 items); "The newer Gen3 inventory consists of 298 sentences" (22 Jan 2024 news item); "sometimes able to build usable voices from substantially fewer than 1600 sentences" (13 Mar 2018 item).
- Fee policy (https://www.modeltalker.org/faq-fee-policy, undated): "You will be charged $100 USD." Registration and recording are free, and the fee applies to download. The page does not waive the fee by diagnosis. Support groups may help.
- Italian: not mentioned. The pages reference only English study eligibility.

**Acapela My Own Voice (Acapela Group, Belgium)**
- Official page (https://mov.acapela-group.com/; © 2026 footer, no launch date): "ONLY 10 MINUTES OF RECORDINGS". "26 LANGUAGES ALREADY AVAILABLE" (list not shown). The page says "The service presents the user with 50 sentences". Requirements: "besides a computer, a headset and an internet connection." Pricing: "the service is free, until you decide to use the voice in a specific application." Paid use is for SAPI on Windows, Google TTS API on Android, or partner applications. No prices are stated.
- Italian: Acapela press release, 29 Jun 2016 (https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/disturbo-della-parola-my-own-voice-la-soluzione-di-sostituzione-vocale-di-acapela-group-e-ora-disponibile-in-italiano-584805241.html, Italian text): "Siamo ora felici di aggiungere l'italiano alla gamma delle dieci lingue già supportate". Creation is free in the initial phase. Recordings are made at home or at the speech therapist's office. Current Italian availability: not verified, because the 2026 page does not list languages.
- Other snippets, not opened: MND Association comparison chart Oct 2025 (PDF unreadable, see section 8); a Tobii Dynavox post dated July 2026 says My Own Voice is included at no extra cost with qualifying Tobii Dynavox devices; a 2023 TechCrunch piece says 50 sentences took 10–20 minutes on Acapela's v3 materials.

**SpeakUnique (UK)**

The page says "those with impaired or unintelligible speech" are served. Registration is "through our website using a laptop and headset microphone". "Funding is also available for our synthetic voices" (prices page not opened). Devices: "Apple and Android devices through our SpeakUnique app", plus Windows communication devices. Voice Design is listed at £300 or £350. These are not verified on the official site.

## 3. Clinical and association guidance

**UK RCSLT (https://rcslt.org/speech-and-language-therapy/clinical-information/voice-banking; undated; newest dated content 2020; page footer © 2026)**
- "Voice banking and message banking are best carried out before there is significant change to speech quality."
- "patients can normally record and create a PSV without delay or cost". Charges come when the voice is loaded onto a device.
- SLT presence is not required: "It is not essential for the SLT to be present for recordings." The SLT "may train the patient to record independently or a significant other to support". The MND Association has trained Association Visitors in some areas.
- Fatigue is listed among the factors in recording time. No session-length advice is given.
- For impaired speech, the SLT "could discuss using a 'donor' voice to create a PSV."

**UK MND Association (https://www.mndassociation.org/)**
- Search summaries of its voice-banking pages say to start as early as possible and that recordings made before speech is seriously affected tend to work better. They also say "keep visits short" and "shorten sessions so the person can rest". One contributor reported about 150 sentences per sitting. These are not fully verified because the PDFs were unreadable (section 8).
- Its Oct 2025 comparison chart (PDF) lists ElevenLabs, Acapela, SpeakUnique and an Apple support article. Its cells could not be read.

**UK NHS (https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/motor-neurone-disease/, last reviewed 19 Feb 2025)**: no voice-banking text. It says an SLT "can advise about suitable communication aids" and that people may need "assistive technology to help with speech" as the condition progresses.

**ALS Association (US)**: official pages were blocked (403). Search summaries of its pages say to start "as soon as possible after diagnosis, while they still have their strongest voice possible", and that voice banking is often not covered by insurance. Not verified from the page itself.

**ASHA (US)**: no official ASHA document on voice banking was found. Snippets from ALS and clinic sources say mildly slurred speech does not rule out a voice bank if the person has enough stamina, and message banking is a less taxing fallback. Not verified from ASHA.

**Italy**
- AISLA: the MY VOICE page (https://www.aisla.it/progetto-my-voice/) gives no clinical guidance on session length or fatigue. Its guideline files were not opened.
- Centro Clinico NeMO: no official clinical guidance was read. Only the NeMO-MY VOICE app and campaign coverage (snippets) were seen.
- Mupin information sheets on Voice for Purpose (snippets, PDFs not opened): they say recording "while speech is still largely intact", and that a donated voice is used only if the person agrees.

**Vendor clinical claims** are not evidence. Respeecher's blog says there are "no proven methods" to treat neurodegenerative disease and claims "patients can completely restore their voices". RCSLT, by contrast, warns against overclaiming.

## 4. Research, 2023–2026

- **Jeon et al., POSTECH, Interspeech 2025 (ISCA abstract, https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2025/jeon25_interspeech.html; arXiv 2508.10412v2, 25 Sep 2025).** Zero-shot multi-speaker TTS for dysarthric speakers, cloned from a single reference recording. Training data is LibriSpeech and evaluation is UASpeech. Model has 33M parameters. Table 1 (arXiv): proposed model PER 14.254 versus Hybrid 31.017 and Conditional 47.696. Speaker similarity 0.619 for the proposed model, versus 0.647 for Conditional (higher). MOS-Nat 3.601 (CI ±0.18) and MOS-Spk 3.909 (±0.21), from 19 MTurk raters. The paper says "dysarthric speech samples are often limited to single-word utterances". WER is not reported. The main claim is intelligibility gains "does not come at the cost of speaker similarity".
- **Samsung R&D India, Interspeech 2025 (blog, https://research.samsung.com/blog/-INTERSPEECH-2025-Fairness-in-Dysarthric-Speech-Synthesis-...; date not shown).** F5-TTS zero-shot on TORGO, with a "few seconds" prompt. Speaker similarity (SIM-o) "remain[s] relatively stable across different severity levels". Intelligibility (ΔWER/ΔCER) is worse for mid and high severity: "Intelligibility is compromised in high severity cases". Prosody similarity falls with severity.
- **Székely et al., Interspeech 2025, "Voice Reconstruction through Large-Scale TTS Models: Comparing Zero-Shot and Fine-tuning Approaches" (ISCA abstract, https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2025/szekely25_interspeech.html).** Compares zero-shot and fine-tuned large TTS across data-availability scenarios. Includes a case study of a stroke survivor with dysarthria. The abstract gives no numbers, and the full paper was not read.
- **Google, zero-shot cross-lingual voice transfer (arXiv 2409.13910, submitted 20 Sep 2024, comment "Submitted to ICASSP").** Average similarity 73% across nine target languages from one English reference. Claims the method "can also work from dysarthric speech". Misuse: "the risk of misuse is significantly lower for individuals who have never had typical speech patterns", and outputs are watermarked. Submission status is not confirmed as accepted.
- **Singh et al., arXiv 2606.19823 (submitted 18 Jun 2026; comment says accepted to Interspeech 2026).** Uses Higgs Audio V2 zero-shot cloning of TORGO speakers for dysarthric ASR data augmentation. WER: zero-shot baseline 31.62%, Clone FT 26.00%, Real FT 24.44%, Hybrid FT 25.12%. Clone FT was best cross-corpus on SAP-1102 (11.45% relative, baseline not named). This is ASR, not a voice-identity evaluation.
- **Other snippets, not opened:** El Hajal et al., arXiv 2501.10256 (rhythm and voice conversion, ICASSP 2025 satellite workshop). Illa et al., arXiv 2106.08427 (2021). IEEE document 9508522 (hybrid VC and TTS for four ALS speakers, claimed 2–3 times closer to the ALS target than to the TTS source; year not verified; the fetch returned no content). An arXiv preprint 2506.04397 (large speech model for voice reconstruction, ALS and cerebral palsy) could not be read. A Scientific Reports article (Jan 2025, via DOAJ) was not reachable.
- **No Interspeech 2024 or ICASSP 2026 dysarthric voice-cloning paper was found** in searches.
- **Case reports (vendor or press):** AudioShake, March 2025 (Patrick Darling, roughly 10 minutes of clear audio from noisy phone clips; Mike G. cloned from stored videos). Both are vendor case studies, not controlled evaluations. A brain-implant study (fine-tuned StyleTTS 2 on pre-illness podcasts) is snippet-only.

## 5. Consent and prevention of cloning another person's voice

- **Apple.** Only device-owner authentication is documented: Face ID, Touch ID, or passcode. The page limits use to "a voice that sounds like you", "using only your own voice", for "personal, non-commercial use". No spoken identity check is documented. Enforcement is not described.
- **Microsoft.** The verbal statement must be recorded in the training data. Microsoft uses it to verify the speaker "through Speaker Verification" to confirm the voice in the statement matches the training data (transparency note). Limited Access applies, and personal voice may only be used in approved application, media and business cases. Other uses are "prohibited". The API is restricted to approved customers. Watermarks are added. Operators must disclose synthetic speech to users (transparency note, disclosure section).
- **ElevenLabs.** Per its help center mirror (https://elevenlabs.io/docs/help-center/product/voices/voice-cloning/can-i-create-a-professional-voice-clone-of-someone-elses-voice.md): "No. You can only create a Professional Voice Clone of your own voice." "Even with their consent, you cannot clone someone else's voice." "All Professional Voice Clones require a verification process to confirm that the voice belongs to you." Sharing is by private link from the owner's account. The Impact page adds manual verification for patients whose voice has changed, and bars under-18 cloning. The official help article itself returned 403. Search snippets (not opened) describe a 24-hour lockout after failed attempts and say that bypassing verification such as CAPTCHA breaches policy.
- **ModelTalker.** It has a "Proxy Voice Banker" category for recording a voice for a person who cannot bank their own. No explicit consent statement was found on the pages fetched.
- **Acapela MOV.** The page makes no consent statement and does not address creating a voice for someone else.
- **Google research.** The blog says zero-shot systems do not guarantee high fidelity. Misuse risk is described as lower for people who never had typical speech, and watermarking is used.
- **Veritone (formerly VocaliD's domain).** The page says "all you need is their consent" for custom voice cloning. This is a commercial, non-clinical context.
- **Translated (Voice for Purpose).** The page describes no donor consent or usage terms. AISLA and Mupin sheets (snippets) say donors are contacted only when chosen.
- **SpeakUnique.** No consent statement on the homepage.

## 6. Conflicting evidence

- **Apple.** 150 phrases (TechRadar 2023 beta, search summary) versus 15 minutes of audio (Apple Newsroom, 30 Nov 2023) versus no count (Apple Support, 13 Oct 2025).
- **Microsoft.** A 5–90 second sample (overview, Sep 2026) versus at least 10 seconds (transparency note, Jun 2026).
- **Google Relate.** About 1–2 hours of phrases (2021–2023 news) versus 500 phrases (current page). Sign-up is now closed.
- **SpeakUnique.** 150–300 sentences versus minimum 125 sentences (both 2022 secondary sources). Official homepage gives no count.
- **Acapela.** 50 sentences (official, 2026), "ONLY 10 MINUTES" (marketing, 2026), 50 sentences in 10–20 minutes (Acapela v3 materials, snippet), and 350 sentences before v3 (snippet).
- **ModelTalker.** 1600–3200 sentences (2017–2019 news) versus 298 (Gen3, Jan 2024 news). RCSLT cites "often between 350 and 1600".
- **Pricing.** ModelTalker $100 (fee-policy page). Acapela free to create, paid for use (official page). An MND chart snippet gives about £90/€99 per year (not verified from the chart). Knowara snippet gives $99/yr or $999 one-time (not opened).
- **NeMO app.** €2.30 (one outlet) versus free with proceeds to NeMO (another).
- **Voice for Purpose scale.** 250 donors and 12 users at the Feb 2023 pilot (Translated) versus more than 6,000 voices in five languages (GreenMe, undated, unverified date).
- **VocaliD.** Vocal Legacy and blended-voice pages (snippets, from the old domain) versus the current redirect to Veritone, which lists no voice banking.
- **ElevenLabs verification.** Official mirror describes "a verification process" (no method). Third-party summaries describe a live phrase-reading check. The official method is not verified.
- **Microsoft Speak For Me.** Launch "later this year" (2024 coverage) versus no 2026 status found.
